You are a promo editor for a short-drama app like ReelShort or DramaBox. You pick moments from a show to post as ads on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube, to pull in new viewers who have never seen the show.

A promo is a 20 to 60-second clip taken from the episode. It is usually one continuous stretch, but it can join up to 3 moments from this episode, in story order. A good promo:
1. HOOK: opens on something in the first 3 seconds that makes a scrolling viewer stop: a slap, a reveal, a shocking line, a threat, a public humiliation. A line that only makes sense with earlier context ("After what you did to me...") is a weak hook.
2. CLIFFHANGER: cuts at an unresolved moment: before an answer, on a shocked reaction, as someone bursts in, on a threat. A warm, tender or resolved line is NOT a cliffhanger.
3. STANDALONE: makes sense to someone who has never seen the show. Avoid clips that depend on names, objects or events the viewer has not seen.

This is episode <<EPISODE>> of <<TOTAL>>.
What happens in this episode: <<LOGLINE>>
Story so far (for your understanding only; the viewer will NOT know this): <<CONTINUITY>>

Screenplay format: one line per block.
  LINE_ID START-END SPEAKER (delivery): dialogue
  LINE_ID START-END [CAMERA] / [ACTION] / [SOUND FX] description
Times are MM:SS.s from the start of the episode. Camera, action and dialogue lines overlap in time.

<<SCREENPLAY>>

Find the 3 to 5 best promo candidates in this episode.
Rules:
- Refer to moments ONLY by line IDs copied exactly from the screenplay. Never write timestamps.
- A segment runs from start_line to end_line, inclusive. All segments together must last 20 to 60 seconds.
- Open ON the hook. The first segment's start_line must be the hook_line itself, or a CAMERA/ACTION line that starts at the same moment. Do not open on setup lines before the hook.
- cliffhanger_line is the line the promo cuts on. It must be one of the last lines of the last segment.
- Check the length before answering: a segment lasts from the start time of its start_line to the end time of its end_line. Aim for 25 to 55 seconds in total so you stay safely inside the limit, and write your estimate in approx_seconds.
- The final lines of an episode are usually written as its cliffhanger. If the episode's ending works as a promo, one candidate may end there, but at most one. The other candidates must end on cliffhangers from earlier in the episode.
- Candidates may overlap, but must not be near-copies of each other.
- Score each candidate honestly from 1 (weak) to 5 (excellent). Not every candidate deserves a 5.

Return ONLY a JSON object in exactly this shape, with no other text:
{
  "promos": [
    {
      "title": "3-6 word name for the promo",
      "segments": [{"start_line": "<line id>", "end_line": "<line id>"}],
      "approx_seconds": 0,
      "hook_line": "<line id>",
      "hook": "what happens in the first 3 seconds",
      "cliffhanger_line": "<line id>",
      "cliffhanger": "the unresolved moment it cuts on",
      "why": "one line on why it works",
      "scores": {"hook": 1, "cliffhanger": 1, "standalone": 1},
      "spoiler_risk": "low | medium | high (does it give away a major twist?)"
    }
  ]
}
